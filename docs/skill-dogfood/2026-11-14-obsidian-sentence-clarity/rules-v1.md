# Sentence Clarity

> **Scope.** Four rules for the full sentences in a note's paragraphs, in whatever language the note is written. They are advice, not a gate.
>
> They do **not** apply to quotes (keep those verbatim), headings, table cells, or bullet fragments. A style the user asked for wins over these rules.

Each rule removes a place where the reader has to stop and read the sentence again.

1. **The subject does the action.** Make the subject the person or thing that actually performs the verb. Do not give an abstract or lifeless noun an action only a person can do.
   - ✗ "Chaos expects you to remember it." → ✓ "You have to remember the messy parts yourself."
   - ✗ 「數據在訴說一個不同的故事。」 → ✓ 「數據顯示的結果和預期不同。」
2. **Keep words that belong together close.** The subject and its verb, or a noun and the words describing it, should not be separated by a long insertion. If they are, split the sentence in two.
   - ✗ "The guitar sitting in the corner because you are still deciding teaches you zero chords." → ✓ "You are still deciding, so the guitar sits in the corner. You have not learned a single chord."
3. **Keep the connecting words.** Do not drop the words that say how two parts relate (because, so, but, that; 因為、所以、但是; ので、しかし) to make a sentence shorter.
   - ✗ "Sample was small; results unreliable." → ✓ "The sample was small, so the results are unreliable."
4. **Use ordinary word combinations.** Do not stack three or more nouns, do not turn a noun into a verb, and prefer the usual phrase over an invented one.
   - ✗ "user retention improvement strategy review" → ✓ "a review of the strategy for improving user retention"
   - ✗ "Calendar it." → ✓ "Put it on the calendar."

**After drafting**, read each paragraph once against the four rules and rewrite every sentence that breaks one. Never shorten a sentence in a way that breaks a rule: a longer sentence the reader understands in one pass is the better one.

## Source and limits

The rules come from the hard-to-read patterns measured in Clark, Martin & Mahowald (2026), "Why is AI-generated language so hard to understand?", arXiv:2610.29162 — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29162>. That paper measured English text only and did not test whether writing instructions reduce the patterns, so the effect of these rules on a note is unproven.
